## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: Brazilian adults who already understand some English (roughly A2 to C1) and want to become fluent without "studying" — professionals who need English at work (meetings, email, docs), developers, university students, and people preparing for interviews, exchange programs or a move abroad. They spend hours a day in a browser (YouTube, Google, ChatGPT, Gmail, WhatsApp Web, social media) almost entirely in Portuguese, have usually tried courses or lesson apps and dropped them.

Secondary: learners of English with any other native language (the extension supports 18+ native languages); served by the English version of the site. Other low-proficiency markets (e.g. Mexico, Colombia, Türkiye) are future targets, not current ones.

Language handling (user request): the site detects the visitor's language on arrival; Portuguese speakers get the PT-BR page, everyone else the English page, with a visible switch.

Context: Brazil sits in the "low proficiency" band of the EF English Proficiency Index 2025 (score 482) — a large audience that understands some English but rarely uses it. (Research note for positioning; not a claim to print without its source.)

## Product Purpose

Active Immersion is a browser extension (Chrome and other Chromium browsers, Firefox) that turns the internet a person already uses into English immersion: it hides or translates their native language while they browse, corrects the English they write, and turns new words into short daily reviews. Success for the site: the visitor installs the extension and starts the 7-day free trial.

## Positioning

It does not add a new app or a study session. It changes the default of the browser: the native language gets out of the way (blurred, translated or removed — including YouTube auto-dubbed videos), every English message the person actually sends (ChatGPT, Gmail, WhatsApp Web…) becomes a correction with the why, and words met in real reading become spaced-repetition cards. Video-only tools (Language Reactor) and word-swapping tools (Toucan) cover one slice; lesson apps create a separate habit people abandon.

## Operating Context

- Install from the Chrome Web Store (https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/active-immersion-learn-en/edgnngnpnkdgcblhegfmedigdnfahegf) or Firefox Add-ons (https://addons.mozilla.org/firefox/addon/active-immersion/). Desktop browsers only; no mobile app.
- Onboarding: language, English level, immersion level (None / Partial / Total), account by 6-digit email code. The 7-day trial starts when the account is created.
- Daily use: browse normally; missions (read, write, review), streaks, XP; a 2-minute practice tour inside the extension.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Hides the native language on pages (blur, translate or remove, by level); removes YouTube auto-dubbed videos; suggests English searches.
- Double-click any English word: meaning, pronunciation, save with the sentence.
- Writing Coach: score, corrections and explanations for English messages sent on any site (never passwords, forms, banks or government sites).
- Spaced-repetition reviews (FSRS), daily missions, streaks, achievements, Insights.
- Breaks with intention: stepping down asks for how long and makes you breathe first; breaks end on their own; commitment mode; one-click "allow this site".
- Interface in 19 languages.
- Pricing: no free plan. 7 days free with everything, no card; then R$ 19,90/month in Brazil or US$ 4.99/month elsewhere, through Stripe; cancel anytime. Subscribing during the trial keeps the remaining trial days.
- No personal OpenAI key; AI runs on the account (daily limits: 50 in trial, 300 on Pro).

## Product Principles

- Change the default, not the schedule: learning happens inside what people already do.
- Honest by design: no invented proof; the trial and the price are stated plainly.
- Friction only where it builds the habit (breaks), never where the person needs their language (one click to allow a site).
- Privacy first: no ads, no tracking, text sent to AI only for the features that need it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Readable at mobile width (visitors arrive from phones and must be able to send the link to their computer); respect reduced-motion preferences; keyboard-reachable demos and CTAs.
